# Creative QA, rights, and delivery checks

Use this reference for work that may be published, sold, advertised, or used
to represent a real person, product, or brand. It is production guidance, not
legal advice. Platform rules and applicable law must be checked against current
official sources at delivery time.

## Universal QA

Review every candidate at full size and at the smallest intended display size:

- brief/subject adherence;
- composition, crop, focal point, and safe area;
- anatomy, geometry, reflections, hands, repeated objects, and visible model
  artifacts;
- color, contrast, sharpness, banding, transparency, and compression;
- legibility and spelling of any text;
- consistency with approved references and prior selected variants;
- dimensions, aspect ratio, file type, and naming;
- alt text or an image description when the asset carries meaning.

A model output that looks plausible is not proof that every visible fact is
correct.

## Fixed facts versus creative degrees of freedom

Before generation, list facts that must not change and choices that may change.
For a product, fixed facts can include SKU, shape, dimensions, material,
finish, label, logo, color, included quantity, and controls. For a person,
fixed facts can include identity, age range, clothing, and requested pose. For a
brand, fixed facts can include palette, logo treatment, typography treatment,
tone, and prohibited visual motifs.

Use an editing/compositing workflow when the fixed facts matter. Do not ask a
model to redraw approved packaging, label text, certification marks, barcodes,
ratings, testimonials, or logos when the source asset can be preserved or
composited.

## Specialist checks

### Portraits and group images

- Review every subject independently for face shape, eyes, hair, skin tone,
  expression, hands, accessories, and unintended identity drift.
- Verify permission for each recognizable person and record the input order and
  intended placement in a group image.
- Label fictional or reconstructed scenes so they cannot be mistaken for
  documentary evidence, an official portrait, or proof of a relationship.

### Logos and brand identity

- Check exact spelling, letterforms, spacing, contrast, one-color behavior,
  small-size legibility, and accidental symbols.
- Separate generated concept art from approved wordmarks, vector files, fonts,
  registered marks, and final production layouts.
- Require human design review and trademark/originality review before adoption;
  visual plausibility is not clearance.

### Virtual try-on and product placement

- Compare the person and item sources separately: face/body continuity,
  garment cut, material, pattern, color, seams, closures, logos, and item count.
- Label the output as a visualization and do not present it as proof of fit,
  size, comfort, fabric performance, or exact color.
- Review skin, hair, hands, occlusion, shadows, reflections, and product edges
  for artifacts introduced by the placement.

### Photo restoration

- Keep the untouched original and identify which regions were cleaned,
  colorized, upscaled, or reconstructed.
- Check faces, text, dates, clothing, objects, architecture, grain, and color
  against the source; do not call invented detail recovered fact.
- Do not use a restored image as forensic, historical-authenticity, or identity
  evidence without qualified human review.

### Interior redesign and staging

- Check walls, doors, windows, floors, fixtures, perspective, furniture scale,
  reflections, and structural boundaries against the source.
- Confirm that staging did not remove safety features or imply measurements,
  availability, property condition, or renovation feasibility.
- Mark altered images as visualization/concept when they could be mistaken for
  an unmodified listing photograph.

### Storyboards and multi-angle sets

- Review frames or angles against one continuity sheet: identity, wardrobe,
  product geometry, props, location, palette, light direction, lens grammar,
  eyelines, and screen direction.
- Keep the approved parent visible in the asset record and do not chain later
  branches from a drifted or failed child.
- Label invented hidden surfaces, fictional events, and reconstructed scenes;
  a sequence of plausible frames is not evidence that an event occurred.

### UI and design mockups

- Check hierarchy, alignment, spacing, component reuse, contrast, focus/label
  cues, localization, overflow, and responsive assumptions.
- Treat generated copy, metrics, prices, logos, testimonials, and product
  claims as placeholders until verified.
- Label the output as a visual mockup. It is not functional software, a tested
  interaction, or an accessibility certification.

## Commercial and platform review

- Separate a platform's primary image from secondary, lifestyle, infographic,
  and ad concepts.
- Check current official platform/category/locale requirements for dimensions,
  background, text, overlays, logos, claims, and prohibited content.
- Treat health, medical, wellness, beauty results, supplements, safety,
  financial, legal, environmental, before/after, comparative, discount,
  certification, endorsement, and review claims as approval-sensitive.
- Never invent a badge, star rating, customer quote, expert endorsement,
  certification, discount, or performance result.
- For a real product, ask for a current SKU/reference sheet and approved claim
  language before final commercial imagery.

## Rights and privacy

Track who supplied each reference, its license/ownership state, any model or
property release, the generation model/settings, post-production, and reviewer.
Escalate recognizable people, private property, distinctive artworks,
third-party logos, screenshots, tattoos, uniforms, celebrity/public-figure
lookalikes, and synthetic people who resemble a real person. A generated image
does not automatically remove likeness, trademark, copyright, privacy, or
release concerns.

Do not send private facepacks, brandbooks, or unreleased product images to a
provider unless the user has authorized that use and understands the external
processing boundary.

## Text and localization

For posters, thumbnails, packaging, charts, and UI-like assets, keep generated
text short and treat the model output as a draft. Prefer a separate layout or
post-production step for exact copy, logos, prices, dates, labels, and legal
language. Recheck spelling, line breaks, numerals, reading direction, locale,
and contrast in the final exported asset.

## Delivery

Return the selected asset plus its model, prompt/reference record, dimensions,
status, QA notes, and any upscaling or post-production still required. Do not
call a URL permanent unless it has been downloaded or archived under the
project's approved storage convention.
